Weapon Lab · Blueprint Lab and Gun Kit

Gun Digitizer

A real gun's digital copy, made from its public sources and nothing else. The tools read the manual, measure its figures, build the model, rig every moving part and animate it the way the real gun works. Every number in the copy names the page it came from.

The sample is the M1911A1 pistol, built from one book: FM 23-35, Basic Field Manual: Automatic Pistol, Caliber .45 (War Department, 1940).

3FIGURES

Scale and frame the figures

Built

A figure is cut from its page and stood upright. Its scale comes from a printed scale bar, from a length fact, or from a part another scaled figure also shows. Its frame comes from the bore line and the bolt face. After that, every pixel on it has a place in the gun's own space.

10HANDS

Hands by grip style, on the character

In progress

One skeleton holds every gun, so every clip plays on every character. Each grip style is the hand setup on it:

  • Stance: where the gun sits in front of the eye.
  • Contacts: the face of the gun each palm lies on.
  • Fingers: straight or wrapped, and the trigger finger off the trigger until the shot.
  • Working holds: the rack, the press check, the magazine.
  • Declared allowances: body parts that may press into the gun, each with its reason.

Each hold searches its own turn, and a hand arcs round the gun between holds. The character's glove is counted where the palm sits, in how far the fingers close and in the clearance allowed.

Step 0 in detail

The character comes first

The character profile is the hand step's first input. It sets:

  • Arms: the arms mesh on the shared skeleton, and whether the character is right- or left-handed.
  • Hand size: palm width and finger length, which set how far a hand reaches round a grip.
  • Hand covering: bare skin and its tone, fingerless gloves, or full gloves with their material and thickness; and the sleeves.
  • Finish: the skin, glove and sleeve finishes, from Coating Lab.

It comes before any hand clip because it changes what the hands are checked against:

  • A glove adds 2 to 3 mm to every finger and the palm, so clearance and finger closure change.
  • A small hand cannot wrap a large grip the same way.
  • The finish goes on the arms, so every picture shows the character who holds the gun.
Version 1

What it is, and what it is not yet

A visual copy

  • The outside, and every part the player sees move.
  • No manufacturing data: no tolerances, no fire-control geometry, nothing a part could be made from.
  • The sources are flat. Widths come from a plan view when there is one, otherwise from a reasoned guess.
  • Figures disagree by 5 to 10 percent on small features, so outer shapes come from the figure scaled by a printed fact.

Next

  • Finish the hands (step 10): the support fingers wrapping over the firing hand, the working clips, the gloves drawn on the arms, the eye view checked in the game.
  • Fit and feature checks, as Model Lab makes for its AR-15: each part's features one by one, a magazine in an open well, the slide's travel clear of the hammer, the sights in line.
  • Cross-sections for the frame, the magazine and the small parts.
  • The AR-15 from its blueprint, checked against Model Lab's hand-built AR-15.
  • One gun for each action family.
  • Later, the same chain for any object with moving parts, with the gun as one sample.
